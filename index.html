Built-In Study Tools

A handful of practical features to make reading, reviewing, and printing less painful.

Bookmarks & Quick Reference

Click the bookmark icon on any section heading to save a Return to spot (earlier sections auto-collapse when you come back), pin a Bookmark, or flag it to Review later. Everything gathers on each course's Quick Reference page with expandable note previews.

Progress Tracking & Collapsible Notes

Pages automatically track what you've reviewed—and flag pages that are New or Updated since you last read them. Collapse or expand individual sections or whole chapters so long study guides stay easy to navigate.

Interactive Glossaries & Key Terms

Hover or tap underlined terms inside lessons to see their definitions without leaving the page. Every lesson lists its own Key Terms, and full course glossaries support search, category filters, related-term links, and lesson backlinks.

Flexible Print Studio

Prefer paper? Print a single page, a whole section, or an entire course in one shot—with an optional glossary appendix, customizable in-text term highlighting, print-only font choices, and multi-column glossary study sheets.

Display & Accessibility Controls

Open Aa Display in the top bar anytime for Light, Dark, and High Contrast themes, dyslexia-friendly and local system fonts (OpenDyslexic, Atkinson Hyperlegible, Lexend), custom text sizing, spacing, line width, capitalization, a customizable Reading Guide, and a Screen Magnifier.

Offline-Ready & Installable

Add {{ site.title }} to your phone's home screen or desktop like an app. Courses you open are saved automatically for offline studying and update quietly when you're back online—without filling your storage with courses you never use.

Why this site exists

Class notes take a lot of time to put together, and usually end up buried in a random folder once the semester ends. Keeping them here makes them easy to pull up from anywhere—and hopefully helps somebody else taking the same class.

Spot a mistake, want to add a topic, or have notes for another class? Everything is plain Markdown on GitHub, and new courses automatically get glossaries, bookmarks, print previews, and offline support.
